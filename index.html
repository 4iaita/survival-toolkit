How the cost is calculated
  • Each anvil job costs left penalty + right penalty + enchantment cost. The penalty is 2ⁿ − 1, where n is how many times that item or book has been through an anvil. The result keeps the higher count plus one.
  • Enchantment cost is the level of each enchantment on the right-hand book times its book multiplier, shown under each enchantment. Bedrock only charges for levels the left item gains, so with fresh books both editions charge the same here. Impaling is cheaper on Bedrock (1 per level instead of 2).
  • In Survival, a job costing 40 levels or more is refused as “Too Expensive!”. The planner never suggests one unless Creative mode is on.
  • XP points assume you pay each job right at that level, for example by leveling up between jobs. If you pay every job from one big pool of levels, only the total levels matter, so pick “Fewest levels”.
  • Assumes one fresh book per enchantment (from the enchanting table, a librarian or loot) with no anvil uses yet. Enchantments already on the item can be left unchecked; set the item’s previous anvil uses instead.
  • The search tries every possible merge order, so the plan shown is the true minimum, not a rule of thumb.
  • Icons are original pixel art drawn for this page.

How brewing works
  • Each step puts one ingredient in the top slot and brews up to three bottles below it. A step takes 20 seconds and uses one charge of fuel; one blaze powder gives 20 charges.
  • Most potions start as an Awkward Potion: water bottles brewed with nether wart. Weakness is the exception and starts straight from water bottles.
  • Redstone Dust makes the effect last longer. Glowstone Dust makes it stronger but shorter. A potion takes one or the other, not both.
  • A Fermented Spider Eye corrupts a potion into another one, such as Swiftness into Slowness or Night Vision into Invisibility.
  • Gunpowder turns a potion into a splash potion with the same duration. Dragon’s Breath turns a splash potion into a lingering one, whose cloud gives a quarter of the duration.
  • On Bedrock, extended Regeneration and Poison last 2:00 instead of 1:30, and Poison II lasts 0:22 instead of 0:21.

Villager

How trading works
  • A villager gets its profession from the job site block it claims, such as a Lectern for a Librarian. Nitwits never trade.
  • Trading gives the villager experience. Each time it levels up (Novice, Apprentice, Journeyman, Expert, Master) it unlocks trades from the next level.
  • A villager offers only some of each level’s trades. Java picks two at random when a level lists more; Bedrock fills each trade slot from its own pool.
  • Each trade can be used a limited number of times, then it locks until the villager restocks at its job site block, up to twice a day.
  • Prices go up when a trade is used a lot or villagers are hurt, and go down after you cure a zombie villager or while you have Hero of the Village.
  • Enchanted book prices depend on the enchantment and its level. Mending and other treasure enchantments cost more.
