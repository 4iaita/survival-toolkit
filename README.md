# Survival Toolkit

Planners for the fiddly parts of Minecraft survival, for Java and Bedrock:

- **Anvil Planner**: pick an item and the enchantments you want. It finds the anvil order that costs the fewest XP points (or levels), without any job hitting "Too Expensive!". It searches every possible merge order, so the plan is the true minimum, not a rule of thumb.
- **Brewing Guide**: pick a potion, how strong it should be and the bottle type. It lists every brewing step, what goes into the stand, and how many ingredients and blaze powder you need.
- **Villager Trades**: pick a villager to see everything it buys and sells at each level, or search for an item (or an enchantment like Mending) to find who trades it.
- **Mobs**: every mob and boss you can meet in survival, in 3D. Pick one to see it up close and read about it. Slimes and magma cubes come in three sizes. Every mob that has babies in the game can be shown as a baby, in the pack's baby look, and so can the parrot, the frog, the cod, the salmon and the tropical fish, 1 in 5 of which the pack makes babies. The wolf comes in all nine biome variants, each shown in the biome it spawns in (pale in the taiga, ashen in the snowy taiga, striped in the wooded badlands and so on), its pup, its angry looks and its Hellhound looks too. The camel, the camel husk and the pig come with or without a saddle (the pig in any of its climates). The horse comes in any of its seven coats, with any of its five markings (a second texture the pack lays over the coat, on the foal and the Wendigo looks too), saddled or not, and in any of the six horse armors (leather in any dye color, or undyed brown). The donkey and the mule can be saddled and carry chests, and the skeleton and zombie horses can be saddled (the zombie horse can wear any of the horse's armor too). The nautilus and the zombie nautilus can be saddled and wear any of the five nautilus armors (copper, iron, gold, diamond or netherite). The llama comes in any of its four coats, on a savanna plateau, and can carry chests and wear a carpet of any dye color (the pack's carpets are a blanket, a harness and leg bands), and so can the trader llama, whose carpet takes the place of its trader's blanket (or its Snowy blanket, or its Halloween fortune teller's costume). The sheep's wool comes in any of the 16 dye colors (with how often sheep spawn in the natural ones), sheared or not. Baby looks, named ones too (the Zombified Piglet, the Baby Mummy, the Ghost Lamb, the Wendigo Foal), can't be saddled, sheared, armored or given a chest. The goat comes with any of the eight horn shapes the pack picks from, or with one horn or none, as it is after ramming a block, and stands on jagged peaks: bare stone mountains under snow, with crags, ice and powder snow. ## Run it

Open `index.html` in a browser. There is no build step needed to use it.

## Edit it

The source lives in `src/`:

- `solver.js`: enchantment data and the anvil merge-order search
- `brew-data.js`: potion recipes and durations
- `trade-data.js`: villager trades by profession and level, the kinds an item can come in (colors, tipped arrows, boat woods) and the enchantments villager gear can carry
- `icons.js` … `icons4.js`: original 16×16 pixel-art icons, drawn from text templates (used where the texture pack has no match)
- `art.js`, `icons5.js`: texture pack icons and which item uses which texture, including job site blocks and village biomes
- `villager.js`: the villager scene: the pack's villager model and skins for each biome and profession, beside its job site block as a 3D block model, turned together by dragging
- `backdrop.js`: each biome's countryside behind the villager scene (hills, trees, a village building and plants), and the forest, cave, nether, End, dragon's island, underwater, lush cave and flooded cave scenes behind the mobs, and the wolf variants' wild biomes (snowy taiga, grove, old growth pine and spruce taigas, sparse jungle, savanna plateau, wooded badlands), the goat's jagged peaks, the warm frog's mangrove swamp, the tadpole's swamp water (under lily pads, over mud and seagrass), built from block textures
- `mob-models.js`: the pack's mob and prop models and how each mob looks with each name (written by `import_models.py`, see below)
- `mobs.js`: draws those models with the villager scene's renderer, plays their animations, and the stage the Mobs and Armor Stands tabs show them on
- `mob-data.js`: the mobs and bosses: their groups (the End's mobs have their own), names and stages, which have babies and which come in variants
- `tag-data.js`: name tags: what each name does, by mob
- `prop-data.js`: the armor stand props: the decoration each item becomes
- `ui.js`, `brew-ui.js`, `trade-ui.js`, `mob-ui.js`, `prop-ui.js`: the five tools and the tool tabs
- `body.html`, `brew.html`, `trade.html`, `mob.html`, `prop.html` and the matching `.css` files: the page

After editing, rebuild the page and the site icons:

```bash
python3 build.py
```

## Credits

Item and block textures in `art/` come from my own texture pack (Bedrock layout, kept next to this project in `../textures`). Every icon uses them: items, blocks (arranged into isometric icons), enchantments, villagers (skins in `art/villager/`, drawn on the 3D villager model), the page backdrop and the site icon (the chest). The page recolors potion liquid and tipped arrow heads for each effect. Only what the pack doesn’t have (the shield, banner and the arrow between trade slots) uses original pixel art drawn from templates in `src/`.

To pick up changes to the pack, copy its textures into `art/` and rebuild:

```bash
python3 import_textures.py ../textures
python3 build.py
```

The name map from pack files to icons is in `import_textures.py`.

The Mobs tab's mobs and the Armor Stands tab's props come from the Actions & Stuff pack itself (`.mcpack`), which keeps its entities, render controllers, models and animations in packed `__brarchive` files under scrambled names. `import_models.py` reads them and runs the pack's own entity scripts and render controllers (with `molang.py`, a small Molang interpreter) once with no name and once for each name the pack checks (mobs that react to no name are listed in `NAMELESS_MOBS`; the tadpole isn't in the pack, so its small model and its swim are written out there, and `import_tadpole.py` puts them on the page by themselves), to find which model, textures and parts each look uses. It writes `src/mob-models.js` and copies the textures from `../textures` into `art/mobs/`, along with the particles it draws (`STAGE_PARTICLES`: a sleeping wolf's Zs). With an unpacked pack it also takes textures from the pack's own folders. After updating the pack, run it again with the pack's path (the `.mcpack`, or the folder it was unpacked into), then rebuild:

```bash
python3 import_models.py path/to/actions_and_stuff.mcpack ../textures
python3 build.py
```

To refresh the looks `import_additions.py` already wrote (the `new_*` ones) after the pack or the importer changes, run it with `--refresh`. It builds each of them again from the pack's render controllers and materials: the layers, how each is lit (glowing eyes, emissive texels, glow panels) and which layers only draw round others and so are left out. It keeps their model and texture keys where the pack's geometry and textures are the same, and drops the ones no look uses any more. It leaves the mobs with importers of their own (above) to them. Without `--write` it only lists what would change:

```bash
python3 import_additions.py ../textures/full-pack ../textures --refresh
python3 import_additions.py ../textures/full-pack ../textures --refresh --write
python3 build.py
```
