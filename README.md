# Survival Toolkit

Planners for the fiddly parts of Minecraft survival, for Java and Bedrock:

- **Equipment**: every piece of armor, weapon and tool you can enchant, in any material it comes in, put on a player in 3D: dress them slot by slot (armor, main hand and off hand), in the pack's own armor and stances, standing idle, drawing the sword or swinging the spear round as they take it in hand. They can be Steve or Alex, as the pack draws them, and in Bedrock also any of the pack's eleven skins (Rana, Sapo, Cole, Endie, Shelldon and the rest, which blink) or one of its twelve Morphs (Rana, Pixie, Lucy, Ash, Iris and the rest, each showing the armor the pack lets it). Any armor but the elytra can wear any of the 18 armor trims in any of its 11 materials (glowing, as the pack draws them, and darker on armor of the trim's own material), leather can be dyed any of the 16 colors, the shield can carry the Banners tab's banner (it follows that tab, in each edition's own shield patterns), and anything can be enchanted, to shimmer on the stage and in its slots (one button enchants everything worn). Leather armor there can also go in any of the pack's ten costumes (the pirate, the elf, the dresses, the hazmat suit...), which it turns into with the right armor trim. Your set adds up what's put on: its armor, toughness, knockback and attack. Under the stage, each piece put on has its own block, the last one on top, to read how good it is and how its materials compare, every enchantment that goes on it, what each does and where it comes from, how to craft or upgrade it, every way to repair it, and where it's found or who sells it. Its Anvil plan section takes the enchantments you want and finds the anvil order that costs the fewest XP points (or levels), without any job hitting "Too Expensive!": it searches every possible merge order, so the plan is the true minimum, not a rule of thumb. Its Enchanting table section shows what the table offers the item in its material, or a book, with 0 to 15 bookshelves: each slot's levels and cost in levels and lapis, every enchantment each slot can give at each level with its chance (for any level it shows, or one you pick), and how many come. Pick the enchantment you want to see your best slot, the chance a try, how many tries make it 90% sure, and how often the slots' hints show it; it says plainly when the table can't give it (treasure enchantments, what only a book brings to that item, a level too high) and sends you to the anvil plan with it. The chances are worked out exactly as Java's code does it, from Java 26.3's own enchantment data, summed over every outcome rather than sampled. Or search by item, material or enchantment.
- **Potions**: every potion you can brew, hovering in 3D, in the pack's own bottles with its effect's swirls round it, in any strength (normal, extended, level II) and as a drinkable, splash or lingering potion or a tipped arrow. Pick one to read what it does and who it doesn't work on, how long each version lasts, what it takes and where that comes from, where it's found ready-made and how to make its tipped arrows; its Brewing section lists every brewing step, what goes into the stand, and how many ingredients and blaze powder you need. Or search by potion, effect or ingredient.
- **Banners**: design a banner as a loom makes one, standing in 3D with its flag swaying as the game's does: pick its color, then add any of the 42 patterns in any of the 16 dyes, up to the loom's six layers, and move or take off any layer. Patterns are drawn from each edition's own textures (they differ a little, and Bedrock's four diagonal halves have each other's names), on the pack's cloth. Show it on a shield instead: the pack's shield standing on the stage, with the patterns as each edition draws them on a shield (pictures of their own, at half a banner's size, inside its rim; Bedrock's ominous one is the pack's own shield), its name and tooltip, and a last crafting step in the loom plan; one button puts it in the player's off hand on the Equipment tab and goes there. Its page says what the game calls it, gives a loom plan step by step (the wool, dyes and banner patterns it takes), says how each of the ten banner patterns is made or where it's found (with the loot tables' chances), and what else banners do: copies, shields (and what they keep through repairs), washing layers off, maps. The ominous banner can be shown too (in Bedrock, the pack's own). Or search for a pattern by either edition's name or by what it looks like.
- **Fireworks**: design a firework as a crafting table makes one. Each star takes 1 to 8 dyes (as many as fit beside its other ingredients), a fade in up to 8 more, a shape (small ball, large ball with a fire charge, star with a gold nugget, creeper with any head, burst with a feather), a trail (diamond) and a twinkle (glowstone dust); the rocket holds as many stars as the grid has room for beside its paper and 1 to 3 gunpowder. The rocket and the picked star lie on the stage as items, the star colored as the game colors it; launch it to see it climb on its trail of sparks and burst as the game works it out tick by tick: each shape, the colors, the fade, the trail and the twinkle, with the flash. Its page shows the games' tooltips for the rocket and the star, in each edition's words; a crafting plan with the grid of every star, fade and the rocket, and everything it takes; and what else rockets do: how high and far each flight duration goes, the explosion's and a crossbow's damage by stars, elytra boosting, dispensers, raids. Or search for a shape, an effect or a color to add it.
- **Decorated Pots**: design a decorated pot, standing in 3D in the game's own shape and the pack's textures: give each of its four sides any of the 23 pottery sherds or leave it plain brick, picking a side by clicking it on the pot or with a button under it (which turns the pot to show it), and turn it all round by dragging. Its page shows the game's tooltip (the sides listed back, left, right, front); a crafting plan with the grid, what it takes and which slot puts which sherd on which side of the placed pot; every sherd with what it shows, where it's found (the archaeology site, its suspicious sand or gravel, or a trial chamber's pots) and its chance from both editions' loot tables, the pot's own first; and what else pots do: storing items, wobbling, breaking and shattering them (with a tool or a projectile), what a trial chamber's pots hold. Or search for a sherd by name, by what it shows or by where it's found to put it on the side picked.
- **Villager Trades**: pick a villager to see everything it buys and sells at each level, or search for an item (or an enchantment like Mending) to find who trades it.
- **Mobs**: every mob and boss you can meet in survival, in 3D. Pick one to see it up close and read about it. Slimes and magma cubes come in three sizes, starting at medium (one named Baby is drawn at the smallest whatever its size, as the pack draws it), and sulfur cubes in two. Every mob that has babies in the game can be shown as a baby, in the pack's baby look, and so can the parrot, the frog, the cod, the salmon and the tropical fish, 1 in 5 of which the pack makes babies. The wolf comes in all nine biome variants, each shown in the biome it spawns in (pale in the taiga, ashen in the snowy taiga, striped in the wooded badlands and so on), its pup, its angry looks and its Hellhound looks too. The camel, the camel husk, the pig and the strider come with or without a saddle (the pig in any of its climates). The horse comes in any of its seven coats, with any of its five markings (a second texture the pack lays over the coat, on the foal and the Wendigo looks too), saddled or not, and in any of the six horse armors (leather in any dye color, or undyed brown). The donkey and the mule can be saddled and carry chests, and the skeleton and zombie horses can be saddled (the zombie horse can wear any of the horse's armor too). The nautilus and the zombie nautilus can be saddled and wear any of the five nautilus armors (copper, iron, gold, diamond or netherite). The llama comes in any of its four coats, on a savanna plateau, and can carry chests and wear a carpet of any dye color (the pack's carpets are a blanket, a harness and leg bands), and so can the trader llama, whose carpet takes the place of its trader's blanket (or its Snowy blanket, or its Halloween fortune teller's costume). The sheep's wool comes in any of the 16 dye colors (with how often sheep spawn in the natural ones), sheared or not. Baby looks, named ones too (the Zombified Piglet, the Baby Mummy, the Ghost Lamb, the Wendigo Foal), can't be saddled, sheared, armored or given a chest. The goat comes with any of the eight horn shapes the pack picks from, or with one horn or none, as it is after ramming a block, and stands on jagged peaks: bare stone mountains under snow, with crags, ice and powder snow. ## Run it

Open `index.html` in a browser. There is no build step needed to use it.

## Edit it

The source lives in `src/`:

- `solver.js`: enchantment data and the anvil merge-order search
- `brew-data.js`: potion recipes and durations
- `potion-data.js`: what each potion and effect does, and where potions and ingredients are found
- `table-data.js`: the enchanting table's maths, done exactly: the slots' levels, the enchanting level, and which enchantments come and how many, from Java's enchantment data; and how the table works, in both editions
- `table-book.js`: what the table gives a book at each enchanting level (written by `tools/table-book.js`: run `node tools/table-book.js` after changing `table-data.js`'s numbers, as working it out takes seconds)
- `gear-data.js`: the materials, what each item is for, recipes, repairs, where equipment is found, the armor trims and their templates, and what each enchantment does
- `banner-data.js`: the banner patterns (both editions' names), the banner pattern items and where they're found, and what banners do, on shields too
- `banner-art.js`: the banner being designed and its pictures, as a banner and on a shield (the Equipment tab's shield wears it too)
- `firework-data.js`: the firework colors, shapes (both editions' names), the tooltips' words, the crafting limits, heights and damage, and what rockets do
- `pot-data.js`: the decorated pot's sides and which crafting slot is which, every pottery sherd and where it's found (with its chance), what a trial chamber's pots hold, and what pots do
- `item-stage.js`: the Potions tab's stage: an item hovering in 3D, with its effect's swirls
- `player.js`: the Equipment tab's player: who it is, what's worn and held on their body, and how they stand
- `player-models.js`: the player's bodies, skins and stances, and what each thing looks like worn or held (written by `import_player.py`, see below)
- `trade-data.js`: villager trades by profession and level, the kinds an item can come in (colors, tipped arrows, boat woods) and the enchantments villager gear can carry
- `icons.js` … `icons4.js`: original 16×16 pixel-art icons, drawn from text templates (used where the texture pack has no match)
- `art.js`, `icons5.js`: texture pack icons and which item uses which texture, including job site blocks and village biomes
- `villager.js`: the villager scene: the pack's villager model and skins for each biome and profession, beside its job site block as a 3D block model, turned together by dragging
- `backdrop-motion.js`: what moves in the backdrops, drawn on a layer over them (the air bubbles rising in the underwater scenes, the gas bubbles of the sulfur pools, the nether's lava and fires and the deep dark's sculk, candle and lanterns, the clouds, the cave's glinting ores, lantern and dripping water, the mineshaft's torches and drips, the mansion's torches, from the pack's animated textures in `art/frames/`, a light snowfall in the snowy scenes, grains of sand hanging in the desert's and the badlands' air, mist among the taiga's far trees, snow blowing off the peaks, spores rising off the mycelium, wheat swaying in the village, flames and sparks about the trial chamber's spawners and vaults, the stronghold's lava and spawner, the dragon island's end crystals, the end city's rods, stars twinkling at night, and leaves falling from the trees)
- `backdrop.js`: each biome's countryside behind the villager scene (hills, trees, a village building and plants), and the forest, cave, nether, End, dragon's island, underwater, lush cave and flooded cave scenes behind the mobs, and the wolf variants' wild biomes (snowy taiga, grove, old growth pine and spruce taigas, sparse jungle, savanna plateau, wooded badlands), the goat's jagged peaks, the warm frog's mangrove swamp, the tadpole's swamp water (under lily pads, over mud and seagrass), the vex's woodland mansion hall (dark oak walls and pillars, windows onto the forest, bookshelves, lit by torches), the breeze's trial chamber (tuff walls and pillars, copper bulbs, grates, trial spawners and vaults), the silverfish's stronghold portal room (stone bricks, iron doors and bars, bookshelves, the portal's frames and its spawner), the warden's deep dark (an ancient city's deepslate brick pillars and its portal's reinforced deepslate frame, overgrown with sculk, sculk sensors and shriekers, lit by soul lanterns), and nights: the plains' for the phantom, the zombie and skeleton horses and the zombie villager (darkened under stars and the moon, the house's windows lit), the pale garden's for the creaking, the swamp's for the bogged and the witch, the desert's for the husk, the parched and the camel husk, the snowy village's for the stray (and any Snowy name on these), and the ocean's for the drowned and the zombie nautilus (dim moonlight from a dark surface), built from block textures
- `mob-models.js`: the pack's mob and prop models and how each mob looks with each name (written by `import_models.py`, see below)
- `mobs.js`: draws those models with the villager scene's renderer, plays their animations, and the stage the Mobs and Armor Stands tabs show them on
- `particles.js`: the pack's particles on the armor stand props' stage
- `mob-data.js`: the mobs and bosses: their groups (the End's mobs have their own), names and stages, which have babies and which come in variants
- `tag-data.js`: name tags: what each name does, by mob
- `prop-data.js`, `prop-data-more.js`: the armor stand props: the decoration each item becomes, the items' own icons, the names of the props' variants (and of the flag's and the stockings' boots) and how to switch between them
- `ui.js`: what the tabs share
- `gear-ui.js` with `anvil-ui.js` (its Anvil plan section) and `table-ui.js` (its Enchanting table section), `potion-ui.js`, `banner-ui.js`, `firework-ui.js`, `pot-ui.js`, `trade-ui.js`, `mob-ui.js`, `prop-ui.js`: the eight tools
- `tabs.js`: the tool tabs
- `body.html`, `gear.html`, `potion.html`, `banner.html`, `firework.html`, `pot.html`, `trade.html`, `mob.html`, `prop.html` and the matching `.css` files: the page

After editing, rebuild the page and the site icons:

```bash
python3 build.py
```

The shield is drawn from the texture of its 3D model, the banners from the pack's own banner model, the Banners tab's banners from the pack's cloth and ominous banner with each edition's own pattern shapes (the pack has none: `import_banner_patterns.py` takes them from Mojang's bedrock-samples and misode's mcmeta into `art/banners/`), and its shields from the pack's shield and ominous shield with each edition's own shield patterns (from the same two places), the firework star from the pack's one texture split into its ball and the middle the game colors, the fireworks' sparks and flash from the pack's particle atlas, the enchanting table's icon from the pack's table textures, the decorated pot from the pack's pot textures and pottery patterns (`import_pots.py` copies them into `art/pots/`), and the leather tunic, which the pack only has in the pale tones the game colors, takes the other leather pieces' browns. Only the arrow between slots uses original pixel art drawn from templates in `src/`.

It keeps their model and texture keys where the pack's geometry and textures are the same, and drops the ones no look uses any more. It leaves the mobs with importers of their own (above) to them. ```bash
